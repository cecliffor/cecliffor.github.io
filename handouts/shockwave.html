What Is Shockwave Therapy?

Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) uses focused or radial acoustic pulses delivered through the skin to stimulate the body's own repair processes. It requires no incisions, injections, or medication. It is best studied for chronic plantar fasciitis (heel pain) and Achilles tendon pain that has not improved after several months of standard care. It is also being used, with growing research, for slow-healing wounds, slow-healing fractures, and circulation-related leg pain.

How Does It Work?

Researchers believe shockwaves work through several biological effects:

  • Stimulating new small blood vessel formation and local blood flow
  • Activating collagen production and tissue repair
  • Calming chronic inflammation
  • Changing how pain signals are transmitted in the treated area
  • Supporting bone-forming cells (for slow-healing fractures)

Conditions We Treat

We'll tell you honestly how strong the evidence is for your condition before you start treatment.

Heel & Tendon Pain Strongest evidence

  • Chronic plantar fasciitis / heel pain
  • Achilles tendinopathy (insertional and mid-portion)

Other Tendon & Forefoot Pain

  • Posterior tibial and peroneal tendon pain
  • Metatarsalgia (ball-of-foot pain)

Healing & Circulation Emerging evidence

  • Diabetic foot ulcers (added to standard wound care)
  • Slow-healing fractures (delayed union / nonunion) and stress fractures
  • Calf pain with walking from peripheral artery disease (claudication)

Nerve Pain Early research

  • Peripheral and diabetic neuropathy
  • Morton's neuroma
  • Tarsal tunnel syndrome

A Note on Nerve Conditions

Laboratory and early clinical studies suggest shockwave therapy may affect nerve function and nerve-related pain, but high-quality trials in neuropathy and neuroma are still limited and results have been mixed. For nerve symptoms, we use shockwave as one part of a broader plan that may include footwear, orthotics, blood sugar management, medications, injections, or surgical options, and we track your response closely.

What the Research Shows

69% vs 35% Average reduction in heel pain with shockwave vs. placebo in a 250-patient, double-blind, multicenter trial for chronic plantar fasciitis.
3–6 months Shockwave gave better pain relief than cortisone injections for plantar fasciitis at 3 and 6 months; results were similar by 12 months.
Achilles Results comparable to targeted exercise for mid-portion Achilles pain, possibly better for insertional pain, and best when the two are combined.
Walking distance In a 138-patient double-blind trial for claudication, shockwave improved walking distance and quality of life compared with placebo.

Results vary from person to person. No treatment works for everyone.

What to Expect

During Treatment

After a brief setup, the device is applied to the treatment area for several minutes. Most visits take 15–20 minutes. You'll feel a tapping or pulsing sensation, and intensity can be adjusted for comfort. No anesthesia is needed.

After Treatment

Temporary soreness, redness, swelling, bruising, or brief numbness or tingling in the treated area can occur and usually resolves on its own. Most patients return to normal daily activities the same day. We'll give you specific activity guidance.

Typical Treatment Course

  • Heel and tendon pain: usually about 3 sessions, one week apart
  • Wound, circulation, and bone protocols: different schedules, planned individually
  • Improvement builds over the ~3 months after your last session as tissue heals
  • Works best combined with stretching/strengthening, proper footwear, and orthotics when appropriate

Why Patients Choose Shockwave

  • No incisions, non-surgical, in-office treatment
  • No injections; no anesthesia needed
  • No medication required
  • Minimal downtime: most people resume daily activities the same day
  • Favorable safety record: side effects are usually mild and short-lived
  • An option before surgery for chronic heel and tendon pain

Is Shockwave Therapy Right for You?

You may be a good candidate if:

  • Heel or tendon pain has lasted 3 months or longer
  • Rest, stretching, orthotics, or physical therapy haven't been enough
  • You'd like a non-surgical option, or an alternative to repeated cortisone injections

Shockwave may not be appropriate if you:

  • Are pregnant
  • Have an active infection (including bone infection) or a tumor in the treatment area
  • Have a pacemaker or implanted electronic device near the treatment area
  • Take blood thinners, have a bleeding disorder, or recently had a cortisone injection (needs individual evaluation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shockwave therapy hurt?

Most patients feel a tapping or pulsing sensation that is uncomfortable but tolerable. Intensity can be adjusted during treatment. Temporary soreness, redness, or swelling afterward is common.

How many shockwave sessions will I need?

For heel and tendon pain, a typical course is about three sessions, one week apart. Wound, circulation, and bone protocols differ and are planned individually.

How soon will I notice results?

Some patients notice improvement within a few weeks, but healing continues for about three months after the final session.

Is shockwave therapy covered by insurance?

Coverage varies by plan and condition. We verify your benefits and give you the self-pay price before treatment so there are no surprises.

Can I have shockwave therapy if I take blood thinners?

Possibly. Blood thinners and bleeding disorders require individual evaluation, so tell us about all of your medications at your visit.
